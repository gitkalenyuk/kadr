Kadr models a CapCut-style timeline. These rules explain what the commands do to it — read them once and most surprises disappear. Every rule here is also written into the descriptions of the commands it affects.

Projects, materials, tracks, segments

ObjectIdWhat it is
Projectprj_…One edit: canvas (ratio, width, height, fps, background), settings, materials, tracks, markers, cover. Stored as %APPDATA%\Kadr\projects\prj_…\project.json with a cache\ folder.
Materialmat_…A media file the project uses — imported (media.import) or created (media.generate, tts.speak, sfx.add, stickers). Type video, photo or audio; photos have duration_us 0. Projects link to files, never copy them.
Tracktrk_…A row of the timeline with a type: main_video (exactly one), video_overlay, text, sticker, effect, filter, adjustment, audio. Empty tracks other than the main track disappear.
Segmentseg_…A clip on a track: a target range on the timeline and, for media clips, a source range inside its material, plus all its properties (transform, opacity, volume, speed, filter, effects, animations, keyframes, text style…).
Markermrk_…A labelled time on the ruler; clip edges snap to it.
Jobsexp_…, cap_…, aij_…Background exports, caption/transcription jobs and smart-tool jobs. They live in memory until Kadr restarts.

Ids are opaque: read them from results and from timeline.get; never build them. Segment ids survive project.duplicate, so ids you know from the original also work in the copy.

Time

  • All times and durations are integer microseconds (µs); parameter names end in _us. 1 s = 1000000; one frame at 30 fps = 33333; at 25 fps = 40000.
  • Timeline times are absolute (from 0). Keyframe times are relative to the start of their segment. Transcript words are stored in source time and mapped to the timeline by transcript.get.
  • Omitted at_us usually means “at the playhead” — each project has its own playhead (playback.seek, playhead_us in timeline.get). Agents should pass explicit times.

Track order is compositing order

Tracks are drawn bottom-up: the track with layer 0 (project.tracks[0], normally the main track) is drawn first, each higher layer on top. timeline.get lists tracks the way the timeline shows them — top to bottom — with each track’s layer:

tracks (timeline.get)     layer
sticker                   3      drawn last, on top
text                      2
video_overlay             1
main_video                0      drawn first
audio                     4…     not drawn
  • Filter, effect and adjustment segments on their own tracks change everything below them for their time range. A filter applied to a clip (filter.apply, effect.apply) changes only that clip.
  • New tracks follow CapCut: overlay tracks go above the main and overlay tracks; text, sticker, effect, filter and adjustment tracks go on top of all visual tracks; audio tracks below the existing audio tracks. So a filter added after text sits above it and colours the text too — add filters first, or use filter.apply on clips.
  • hidden tracks are not drawn, muted tracks are not heard, locked tracks reject edits with conflict (timeline.track.set).

The magnetic main track

  • With main_track_magnet on (default), clips on the main track always sit back to back from 0 — there are never gaps. Adding without at_us appends; adding with at_us inserts at the clip boundary nearest to it and ripples later clips right. Deleting, trimming and speed changes ripple too.
  • Don’t fight the magnet. To place a clip at an exact time on the main track, build the sequence in order (batch of appends with duration_us), or turn the magnet off with timeline.settings.set. For free placement use an overlay track (new_track).
  • On other tracks a clip starts exactly at at_us; if that spot is taken, a new track is created directly above.
  • Snapping (auto_snapping) pulls moved and trimmed edges to other edges, the playhead, markers or 0 within 100 ms. Pass snap: false to timeline.segment.move or timeline.segment.trim for exact positions — beat markers are a classic trap.

Segment ranges and speed

  • start_us/duration_us is where the clip sits on the timeline; source_start_us/source_duration_us is the part of the material it plays. At speed s, source_duration = duration × s.
  • Video and audio cannot be longer than their material (edges stop at the media end, results report clamped: true). Photos, text, stickers, effects and filters stretch freely.
  • Default lengths: whole material for video/audio, 5 s for photos, 3 s for text, stickers, effects, filters and adjustment layers, 2 s for a manual caption.
  • Video clips keep their own sound until audio.extract or audio.separate.

Linked clips

Clips that share a link_group (lnk_…) move, trim, split and delete together while linkage is on (default). Links are created by audio.extract / audio.separate (video + its sound) and tts.from_text (text + its speech). timeline.get shows link_group on each clip. Turn linkage off with timeline.settings.set to edit one side alone.

Transitions

  • A transition is stored on the left clip of a cut (transition_out) and plays across the cut into the next clip on the same video track. transition.set takes the left clip’s id.
  • The two clips must touch (within half a frame) — otherwise conflict. Both must be at least 0.2 s long; the duration is clamped to half of the shorter clip (clamped: true).
  • Transitions do not change the timeline length: cuts stay where they are, so narration and music timing are unaffected.
  • transition.apply_all sets one transition on every cut of a track. Directional and soft variants are options (direction, softness) stored in the type, e.g. bars_stagger?direction=up.

Keyframes and easing

  • Animatable properties: transform.x, transform.y, transform.scale, transform.rotation, opacity, volume, adjust.* and filter.intensity.
  • With two or more keyframes a property is interpolated between them; before the first and after the last it holds that value. Keyframe values replace the static property.
  • time_us is relative to the segment start. A keyframe within half a frame of an existing one updates it instead of adding another.
  • The left keyframe of each interval decides the easing: linear (default), or cubic ease_in, ease_out, ease_in_out. Put the easing on the first keyframe of a move.
  • While a property has keyframes, segment.set_transform, segment.set_opacity, segment.set_volume and segment.set_adjust write a keyframe at the playhead instead of changing the static value (listed in result.keyframed). Use keyframe.add with explicit times, or keyframe.clear first.

A slow push-in with an eased start, then a check of the interpolated value at 1 s:

kadrctl run keyframe.add --project_id prj_3c8e1f0a7b62 --segment_id seg_b41d7e0c9a35 --property transform.scale --time_us 4s --value 1.2

Transform and coordinates

  • x and y move the clip’s centre in canvas fractions: 0 = centred, ±1 = centre on an edge (one unit is half the canvas width or height). +X is right, +Y is down. Range −2…2.
  • scale 1 = the clip fits inside the canvas (letterboxed if the shapes differ). To fill a 16:9 canvas with 4:3 footage you need about 1.33; with 9:16 footage about 3.16. A clip at scale s that has the canvas’s shape can move sideways by s − 1 before an edge shows.
  • rotation is in degrees, clockwise (−360…360). Flips are flip_h/flip_v.
  • Stickers start at scale 0.3; text size 15 is CapCut’s default (stroke, shadow and box values are 0–1).
  • To measure, use preview.layout: boxes in canvas pixels for every visible clip at a time.
kadrctl run segment.set_transform --project_id prj_3c8e1f0a7b62 --segment_id seg_0f6b2d8e4c91 --y 0.6 --scale 0.8

Undo, history and scopes

  • Project commands (135 of them) take project_id, edit a working copy, and are saved as one undo step each — or one step per batch. A command that changes nothing creates no step. History is shared by the editor and every client; edit.history shows the next undo/redo labels.
  • Global commands do not belong to one project’s history: projects list/create/duplicate/delete, library, presets, export jobs, captions.auto and transcript.transcribe (jobs; the cues they create are one undo step), tools, updates, ui.toast, ui.library_tab. They cannot be part of a batch.
  • Not undoable: exports, transcripts (analysis data — transcript.clear removes one), project.rename, project.delete, proxy creation and the ui.* commands (they change only what the window shows).
