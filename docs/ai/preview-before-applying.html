Choose a transition and an effect by looking at them

You get a transition and an effect chosen by eye, not by name. Claude narrows the library to a few candidates, renders each one as a contact sheet to see how it moves, applies the best one with the right direction and sliders, and checks the result in your own edit.

  • Time: ~1 min
  • You need: A Kadr project with two clips that touch
  • Main commands: library.list, library.preview, transition.set, preview.contact_sheet, effect.add, preview.frame

Ask Claude

In my Kadr project "Travel teaser", pick a calm, flowing transition for the cut between the two clips — look at a few liquid transitions with library_preview before you decide, and tell me why you chose it. Then add a weather effect over the second clip that suits a rainy evening, again comparing a couple of options visually. Show me a contact sheet of the cut when you're done.

Step by step

Each step shows the same calls twice. The kadrctl tab is for PowerShell: a line that starts with kadrctl --% passes JSON, and without --% the same line also works in Command Prompt. The MCP tab is the tool call an agent sends — the tool name and its arguments. Ids such as prj_… and seg_… come from the results of earlier steps, so yours will differ. In this example the project holds two generated 3-second clips: a sunset mesh gradient and a city-lights bokeh.

  1. Shortlist candidates

    A category tab is the quickest filter: Liquid has 14 transitions. The descriptions say what each one looks like and when to use it.

    kadrctl run library.list --kind transition --category Liquid
    {"name": "library_list", "arguments": {"kind": "transition", "category": "Liquid"}}
    tide_wipe     Tide          A rolling wave front with a sparkling white foam crest washes the new clip in. Great for beach, travel and summer footage…
ink_bleed     Ink bleed     Dark ink blots spread with organic tendrils from a few drops; the ink clears behind its front to reveal the new clip…
paint_splash  Paint splash  A spiky paint splash with flying droplets bursts from near the centre and floods into the new clip…

  2. Look at each one

    Through MCP, library_preview comes back as an image, so Claude actually sees the motion. With kadrctl or REST you get the same PNG as base64 in png_base64.

    A one-row strip of six tiles is enough to compare. The tiles run from clip A (a sunny landscape) to clip B (a night city).

    kadrctl run library.preview --kind transition --id tide_wipe --frames 6 --columns 6 --tile_width 160
kadrctl run library.preview --kind transition --id ink_bleed --frames 6 --columns 6 --tile_width 160
kadrctl run library.preview --kind transition --id paint_splash --frames 6 --columns 6 --tile_width 160
    {"name": "library_preview", "arguments": {"kind": "transition", "id": "tide_wipe", "frames": 6, "columns": 6, "tile_width": 160}}
{"name": "library_preview", "arguments": {"kind": "transition", "id": "ink_bleed", "frames": 6, "columns": 6, "tile_width": 160}}
{"name": "library_preview", "arguments": {"kind": "transition", "id": "paint_splash", "frames": 6, "columns": 6, "tile_width": 160}}

    Tide sweeps a wavy foam line across the frame; Ink bleed grows dark blots that would muddy a bright sunset; Paint splash is loud. For a calm travel teaser Claude picks Tide.

  3. Read its options

    The item from step 1 lists what tide_wipe accepts: "directions": ["right","left","down","up"], "softness": true, "default_softness": 0.2. A wave coming in from the right reads as forward motion, and a slightly softer edge suits the gradient.

  4. Apply it to the cut

    The transition goes on the left clip of the cut. direction is the way the motion travels.

    kadrctl run transition.set --project_id prj_e21b467917e3 --segment_id seg_3ccd6c9e2cbe --type tide_wipe --duration_us 0.8s --direction left --softness 0.3
    {"name": "transition_set", "arguments": {"project_id": "prj_e21b467917e3", "segment_id": "seg_3ccd6c9e2cbe", "type": "tide_wipe", "duration_us": 800000, "direction": "left", "softness": 0.3}}
    type tide_wipe?direction=left&softness=0.3   duration_us 800000   clamped false   max_duration_us 1500000

  5. Check it in the edit

    The library preview used sample pictures; now look at the real cut. Four tiles across the 0.8 s around the cut at 3 s:

    kadrctl run preview.contact_sheet --project_id prj_e21b467917e3 --start_us 2.6s --end_us 3.4s --count 4 --columns 4 --tile_width 240
    {"name": "preview_contact_sheet", "arguments": {"project_id": "prj_e21b467917e3", "start_us": 2600000, "end_us": 3400000, "count": 4, "columns": 4, "tile_width": 240}}

    At 2.7, 2.9, 3.1 and 3.3 s the foam line travels from the right edge to the left, uncovering the city lights.

  6. Do the same for an effect

    Weather has 8 effects. Their items list the sliders in params.schema — rain_glass has density and fog besides intensity and speed. Look at it, then add it over the second clip with denser drops and less fog, and check one frame.

    kadrctl run library.list --kind effect --category Weather
kadrctl run library.preview --kind effect --id rain_glass --frames 4 --columns 4 --tile_width 200
kadrctl --% run effect.add --project_id prj_e21b467917e3 --effect_id rain_glass --at_us 3s --duration_us 3s --params "{\"density\":0.7,\"fog\":0.3}"
kadrctl run preview.frame --project_id prj_e21b467917e3 --time_us 4.5s --width 640
    {"name": "library_list", "arguments": {"kind": "effect", "category": "Weather"}}
{"name": "library_preview", "arguments": {"kind": "effect", "id": "rain_glass", "frames": 4, "columns": 4, "tile_width": 200}}
{"name": "effect_add", "arguments": {"project_id": "prj_e21b467917e3", "effect_id": "rain_glass", "at_us": 3000000, "duration_us": 3000000, "params": {"density": 0.7, "fog": 0.3}}}
{"name": "preview_frame", "arguments": {"project_id": "prj_e21b467917e3", "time_us": 4500000, "width": 640}}

    The effect lands on its own effect track (segment.effect = {"id": "rain_glass", "params": {"density": 0.7, "fog": 0.3, "intensity": 1, "speed": 1}}) and the frame shows raindrops on glass over the city lights.

Variations

  • Clip animations: kind: "animation" (categories In, Out, Combo, Loop), then animation.set. Use frames: 8, columns: 4 for longer moves.
  • Text: text_template, text_effect and text_animation render sample text; apply with text.add, text.apply_effect or text.set_glyph_animation.
  • Filters: the tiles show a before/after divider, which makes subtle grades easy to compare side by side.
  • Captions: caption_style shows the word highlight moving through a sample caption.
