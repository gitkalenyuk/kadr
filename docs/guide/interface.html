Interface tour

The Kadr editor has the same layout as CapCut: library on the left, player in the middle, properties on the right and the timeline at the bottom. Here is what every part does.

Top bar

  1. Menu and Back to home. Menu holds New project, Import media, Undo and Redo (with the name of the step, e.g. “Undo Split”), Keyboard shortcuts, API documentation, Settings and Check for updates. A small dot on Menu means an update is ready. The arrow ← takes you back to the home screen.
  2. Project name. Click it to rename the project, then press Enter.
  3. Shortcuts and Export. Shortcuts lists every keyboard shortcut (Ctrl+/). Export saves your video as a file (Ctrl+E).

Library (4)

Everything you can add to a video lives here, sorted into tabs. Pick a tab at the top, a category on the left, and click a card to preview it. Click + on a card (or double-click it) to add it at the playhead, or drag it onto the timeline. Hover over a card for a moment to see it move: transitions, effects, filters, animations, animated text, stickers and backgrounds play a short preview right in the card.

If some tabs are hidden at the right edge, drag the border between the library and the player to make the library wider, or scroll the tab row sideways.

Player (5)

Shows your video exactly as it will be exported. Press Space to play and pause. You can click a clip in the picture to select it and then move, scale or rotate it with the mouse. Below the picture are the time, the play button, the preview quality, the Ratio menu and full screen. More in Player & preview.

Inspector (6)

The settings of whatever is selected. Select a video clip and you get the tabs Video, Audio, Speed, Animation and Adjustment; select a title and you get Text and Animation; an audio clip shows Basic, Voice changer and Speed. When nothing is selected, the inspector shows the Draft settings of the project: frame rate and canvas background.

Every change is saved and can be undone with Ctrl+Z. Many sliders have a small diamond on the right: that is for keyframes (animating a value over time).

Toolbar (7)

The row above the timeline. From left to right:

  • Select / Split tool (A / B), Undo, Redo;
  • Split, Delete left, Delete right, Delete;
  • Add marker, Freeze frame, Reverse, Mirror, Rotate, Crop;
  • Smart tools — scene split, silence removal, auto reframe, separate audio and auto captions;
  • on the right: Main track magnet, Auto snapping, Linkage, Preview axis, timeline zoom, Zoom to fit and Hide timeline.

Hover over any button to see its name and shortcut. Buttons that don't apply to the current selection are greyed out.

Track headers (8)

Each track row starts with small buttons: a padlock to lock the track (protect it from edits), an eye to hide it and a speaker to mute it. The main video track also has a Cover button for choosing the project's thumbnail.

Timeline (9)

Your video laid out in time, from left to right. The ruler at the top shows the time; click or drag on it to move the playhead (the white vertical line). Tracks are stacked: the lowest video track is the main track, picture-in-picture clips, stickers, text, effects and filters go on tracks above it, and audio goes below. Coloured flags on the ruler are markers. Everything about editing here is in Timeline editing.

Resize the panels

Drag the thin borders between the panels to make the library, inspector or timeline bigger. Press Ctrl+. to hide the timeline and get a large player; press it again to bring it back.

Edited by someone else? If an AI assistant or a script changes your project, the editor updates live and shows a short message such as “Edited via MCP”. Those changes can be undone like your own. See the AI documentation.
