AI video generation

Kadr can ask an AI video service to make a shot for you — an establishing shot for an empty gap, a new take of a clip, a moving version of a photo — and put the result exactly where you need it on the timeline. You bring an account with one of 37 providers (Google Veo, Kling, Seedance, Wan, Hailuo, Runway, Luma, fal.ai, Replicate and many more), Kadr does the rest and always tells you the price first.

AI video generation is optional and paid by you to the provider you choose. Kadr itself stays free and works offline without it.

Set up a provider

  1. Open the settings

    Open Settings → Video generation (or click the gear in the Generate tab). The list shows every provider with its models, price hints and whether you have a working key.

  2. Add a key

    Open a provider, follow the link to its keys page, and paste the key under API keys. Like voices, every provider can hold many keys that are used in order with automatic failover — see Several keys for one provider. Google Vertex AI also accepts a service-account .json file; Kling and others that use two-part keys take them as access-key:secret-key.

  3. Test and choose defaults

    Test checks the key with a free request (providers without a free check say so, and the key is verified on first use). Fill in any setting the provider needs (for example the Google Cloud project and region for Vertex AI, or a regional address), choose a default model, and make the provider your default if you like.

OpenAI shut down the Sora 2 API on 24 September 2026, and Stability AI's video API is gone too, so they are hidden. Google retires the Veo preview model ids on 22 October 2026 — choose a current Veo or Gemini Omni model.

The Generate tab

Open the Generate tab in the library on the left.

  1. Model — search, or filter by what the model can do: Text, Image, First+last frame, reference images (Refs), Video editing, Sound, or only models With key. Badges show which models generate sound and which are retiring.
  2. Mode, when the model has several:
    • Text — describe the shot;
    • Image — animate a start image;
    • First + last — bridge two images;
    • References — keep a character, product or style consistent;
    • Video — restyle or edit a clip.
  3. Prompt — what should happen in the shot: subject, action, camera, light, mood.
  4. Settings — the model's own options: duration, aspect ratio, resolution, frame rate, seed (the dice button picks a random one), negative prompt, camera motion, sound. Advanced options and Extra JSON (raw parameters passed to the provider) are folded away.
  5. References — pick images or clips from Media, a file on your PC, or the current frame under the playhead.
  6. Check the cost at the bottom and click Generate.

Without a timeline target the result goes to Media; switch on Also add to the timeline at the playhead to place it right away.

Generate straight into the timeline

The fastest way: let Kadr fill a gap or replace a clip.

  1. Choose the place

    Select a video clip, or put the playhead in an empty gap of a track. Then click the Generate here wand button in the toolbar, or right-click the clip or the gap and choose Generate here….

  2. Kadr fills in the form

    The Generate tab opens with the target shown at the top. The duration comes from the length of the range and the aspect ratio from your project. Under Suggested for this range Kadr offers the frames around it as references — the last frame of the clip before and the first frame of the clip after — so the new shot can continue the scene.

  3. Decide how it fits

    The model may make a clip that is longer or shorter than the range:

    • Auto — trims when the result is longer, speeds up or slows down slightly when it is a little shorter;
    • Trim — cuts the result to the range at normal speed;
    • Fit speed — changes the speed so the whole result fills the range exactly;
    • Full length — keeps the whole result, even if it runs past the range.

    Mute generated sound is handy for B-roll under a narration.

  4. Generate

    When the job is done the clip lands in the range as one undo step: Ctrl+Z takes it out again. A replaced clip keeps its position, effects, transform and keyframes. The generated file is copied into the project folder, so the project stays complete.

The price, before you pay

When you click Generate for a paid model, Kadr shows a confirmation with the estimated cost, how much you have spent today and your daily limit. Nothing is sent until you confirm.

  • Estimates come from the provider's public prices with their date. Providers that bill in their own credits, or have no public price, show cost unknown — Kadr still asks.
  • An identical request you already made is reused for free. Switch on New take (generate again) to pay for a fresh version.
  • Set a daily limit in Settings → Spending: a generation that would go over it is refused. Running jobs count against the limit.
  • A job cancelled while it is still waiting in the provider's queue is refunded in Kadr's spending history. Some providers cannot cancel a started job and may still bill it — the message says so.

Your real bill comes from the provider. Kadr's numbers are estimates to help you decide and to enforce your limit.

Jobs and history

Below the form is the list of jobs: queued and running ones with a progress bar and Cancel, finished ones with a preview. For a finished job you can:

  • show it on the timeline (selects the clip and moves the playhead to it);
  • place it into the original range again, or add it at the playhead;
  • add it to Media without touching the timeline;
  • edit and retry — load its settings into the form, change the prompt or the model, and run it again.

Generation runs in the background, so you can keep editing. Jobs survive a restart of Kadr: unfinished ones continue when it starts again. If a job fails, its message explains the provider's reason and what to change.

Your own provider

For a service Kadr doesn't know yet, or your own server, open Settings → Video generation and choose Custom provider under Add a provider. Fill in the usual pieces of an asynchronous video API:

  • the base URL and how the key is sent (bearer token, a header, a query parameter);
  • the submit request: method, URL and a JSON body with placeholders — {prompt}, {model}, {duration}, {aspect_ratio}, {resolution}, {seed}, {negative_prompt}, {generate_audio}, {first_frame}, {last_frame}, {reference_images}, {source_video} (leave the body empty to send Kadr's common body);
  • where to find the job id in the answer, the poll URL with {job_id}, the status field and which values mean done, failed or running, the video URL path, and optionally progress, error, result and cancel requests;
  • how reference files are uploaded, the models with their durations, resolutions and ratios, and a price per second if you know it.

Preview request shows exactly how Kadr would call the service for a sample prompt — without sending anything. You can also copy a built-in provider and change only its address (for a proxy or a regional endpoint).

Privacy

Your prompt and the references you choose — images, frames of your video, clips — are uploaded to the provider you picked. Nothing else from your project leaves your PC. Keys are kept in Kadr's encrypted vault and never appear in projects or exports.
